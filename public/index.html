Insure or Not? Working Through Loss-Only Cargo Cover on a China Shipment

Buyers usually decide on shipping insurance just before dispatch by asking "is it worth it?" That cannot be answered in the abstract. JTLGO's current cover pays for loss, not damage, and has a ceiling, so the useful question is narrower: at this value and weight, what does each option return if the cargo disappears? Below is that arithmetic, using only JTLGO's published figures, plus what neither option pays for.

The two options, stated as numbers

JTLGO offers insured and uninsured shipping, and the choice has to be made before the cargo leaves. Once cargo has left, insurance normally cannot be added and the insured amount cannot be increased.

The insured option uses one rate: the premium is 5.5% of the goods value, with a minimum of USD 10 and a maximum of USD 50 per shipment. The insured amount is set at 110% of the actual goods value, capped at USD 1,000. The extra 10% exists because the insurer applies a 10% deductible; setting the insured amount at 110% is meant to cover the insurable value as fully as possible. JTLGO points out that 5.5% of goods value is the same figure as 5% of the insured amount, so there is one rate, not two.

The uninsured option is not "nothing". If all or part of the cargo is confirmed lost, JTLGO compensates per kilogram by channel: USD 6 per kg by air and USD 3 per kg by sea, up to USD 100 per shipment, credited to the customer's JTLGO account balance, where it can offset later purchases and shipping charges.

Both sit on one policy page; read JTLGO's loss-only cargo cover and claim rules in full, because the conditions matter as much as the rates.

Three worked cases

The aim is to see where per-kg compensation stops being enough. These cases are invented to show the method, not quotes.

Case 1: light, valuable air cargo. Goods value USD 500, 4 kg by air. Insured: the premium is USD 500 × 5.5% = USD 27.50 and the insured amount is USD 550. Uninsured: confirmed loss would return 4 × USD 6 = USD 24. For light, high-value goods the per-kg rule protects very little of the value.

Case 2: heavy, cheap sea cargo. Goods value USD 150, 30 kg by sea. Insured: 5.5% of USD 150 is USD 8.25, which is below the minimum, so the USD 10 per-shipment premium applies. Uninsured: 30 × USD 3 = USD 90, under the USD 100 cap. Here the uninsured compensation already approaches the goods value, and the decision is closer.

Case 3: value above the ceiling. Goods value USD 2,000. 110% of the value would be USD 2,200, so the insured amount is capped at USD 1,000 and the premium at USD 50. Value above the cover limit is not insured. JTLGO's threshold is USD 909.09: above that, 110% of the value exceeds USD 1,000. If your goods are worth more than the cap, you have to tell JTLGO before dispatch and assess the uncovered risk yourself.

Two rules apply to every case. The maximum insured amount is an upper limit, not a payout; the insurer decides the final amount from the cover, the deductible and its own investigation, and it never exceeds the loss you can prove. And for uninsured partial loss, the USD 100 limit is per shipment — it is not multiplied by the number of parcels, cartons or products on one waybill.

What loss-only means when a carton arrives crushed

The current cover is loss only: lost cargo is covered, damaged cargo is not. Broken, crushed or deformed outer packaging, moisture, scratches, internal damage and any damage without actual loss are outside the insurance and outside the per-kg compensation.

Damage is handled as a complaint and claim to the actual delivery carrier, and the steps happen at the door. Photograph or film at delivery: the outer packaging, the shipping label, every side of the carton, the damaged areas and the goods after opening. Keep the original packaging and the damaged goods until the local carrier finishes its investigation. If possible, mark "Package Damaged" or "Cargo Damaged" on the delivery receipt or in the carrier's system before signing. Contact the local carrier at once and file before its deadlines expire, then send the same photos, tracking number and details to JTLGO. JTLGO assists with documents and communication, but the carrier decides liability and amount.

This is why packaging belongs in the insurance conversation. The customer and supplier are responsible for packaging suited to international transport. For fragile, precision, high-value or easily deformed goods, ask the supplier to reinforce the packing, or ask JTLGO to assess repacking once goods reach the warehouse. Reinforcement is priced on the warehouse charge sheet, where insurance is also listed as the separately confirmed insurance line on the fee sheet rather than a bundled default.

Exclusions and timing that apply either way

Some losses sit outside both options unless the insurer or carrier agrees otherwise in writing: delay and indirect economic loss; detention, return, destruction or fines caused by customs policy, import restrictions, certification or declaration issues; losses from wrong, incomplete or false information; supplier quality problems or wrong goods; insufficient supplier packaging; concealed or restricted goods shipped without confirmation; wrong address, unreachable recipient, unpaid duties or refused delivery; and destruction from the goods' own condition, such as leakage.

Timing also differs by channel for the uninsured compensation. For air cargo lost or held by customs before the overseas carrier collects it, a refund of freight plus USD 6 per kg can be requested if the cargo still has not been collected 45 calendar days after JTLGO received it. For sea cargo, the same applies at 90 calendar days and USD 3 per kg. If the loss happens after the overseas carrier collects the cargo, freight is not refunded and only the per-kg compensation applies. In the before-collection case, the freight refund is separate and does not count toward the USD 100 cap.

Prepare the claim file early, whichever you choose: the international tracking number, purchase order, commercial invoice, proof of payment, product name, model and quantity, proof of value, packing list, photos or video before dispatch and at delivery, and a list of missing or damaged items. FAQ

Can I add insurance after my parcel has left the warehouse? Normally no. The decision has to be made before dispatch, and the insured amount cannot be increased once the cargo has left.

Does insurance pay if the goods arrive broken? No. The current cover is loss only. Damage is claimed against the local delivery carrier, with JTLGO assisting with documents.

Is the USD 100 uninsured limit per carton? No. It is per shipment. Several parcels or cartons on one waybill share one USD 100 maximum.

When does JTLGO recommend insuring? For high-value cargo, JTLGO recommends buying shipping insurance before dispatch.
